You are a mechanistic interpretability researcher generating Wikipedia-style
sentence-completion prompts for Gemma-2-2B circuit tracing. Your goal: interesting prompts
whose attribution graphs may contain a **surprising supernode** — a concept the
model uses internally that is not directly suggested by the input prompt or
the output token.

The headline example from our exploration is the prompt "Some of the best
candidates for future deep" → space (43.5%): the graph contains an explicit
\textsc{Apollo} supernode (the Apollo space program), despite "Apollo" appearing
nowhere in the prompt or the predicted next word.

---

# Target criterion (verbatim from our automated judge)

> **Surprising supernode.** A supernode represents a concept or association
> that is not directly suggested by the model's input and output. Medium
> scores: associations the model might plausibly make but that aren't a given.
> High scores: genuinely surprising associations.

---

# Design philosophy — avoid retracing the Anthropic biology paper

Anthropic's biology paper (Lindsey et al. 2025) and the original attribution-
graph methods paper (Ameisen et al. 2025) already characterize the canonical
circuits: 2-hop city→state→capital factual recall, entity→sport (Michael
Jordan → basketball), simple two-operand addition, antonym/synonym
substitution, acronym expansion, induction patterns. The intermediate hops
in those cases (state, sport) are not "surprising" — they are the expected
hop. Generate prompts where the hidden concept is genuinely *unexpected*. These prompts should be interesting and expansive, not just "naming the obvious intermediate concept", but should reveal something genuinely worth studying within a model further for a full research paper. Our Apollo supernode should pale in comparison with your generated prompts.

---

# Filter constraints

- 8-30 words in the prompt
- Single-token next word: one English content word, no punctuation
- The expected next word must NOT appear verbatim in the prompt
- The surprising concept must NOT appear in the prompt OR the next token
- top-1 confidence should reasonably be around 0.2 and 0.6

---

# Topic seeds (anchor your prompts in these; cap each at ~1 prompt)

Science & technology, history & civilization, arts & culture, geography &
nature, social sciences, sports & games, music, literature, philosophy.
Cultural and pop-culture domains are especially fertile here (mythology,
brand names, archetypes, idioms) because the hidden supernode is often a
specific named instance.

---

# Output format

Produce exactly 10 prompts. Output ONLY the CSV inside one code fence —

Columns (matching the standard ground_truth CSV schema):
- `slug`: `generated-ss-NNN`
- `prompt`: 8-30 word sentence prefix
- `intermediate_concept`: the surprising concept you expect as a hidden
  supernode, not present in prompt or next token
- `correct_answer`: single most likely next content word
- `hop_type`: always `surprising_supernode`
- `notes`: one sentence justifying why this concept is load-bearing AND
  surprising

After the CSV: 1 paragraph on the distribution of surprising-concept types
(historical instance, cultural archetype, hidden geographical anchor, etc.)
and any patterns you noticed.
