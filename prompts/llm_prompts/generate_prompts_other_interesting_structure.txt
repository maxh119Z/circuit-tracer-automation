You are a mechanistic interpretability researcher generating Wikipedia-style
sentence-completion prompts for Gemma-2-2B circuit tracing. Your goal: interesting prompts
whose attribution graphs may show **structure that doesn't cleanly fit the other
three criteria** (parallel computation, surprising supernode, complex
internal computation) but is still notable — unusual topology, cross-domain
bridging, implicit pragmatics surfacing in the graph, self-referential or
recursive structure, etc.

---

# Target criterion (verbatim from our automated judge)

> **Other interesting structure.** A catch-all for generally interesting
> internal structure. If you notice something notable about the graph that
> doesn't fall into the other criteria, score it high here.

---

# Design philosophy — avoid retracing the Anthropic biology paper

Anthropic's biology paper (Lindsey et al. 2025) and the original attribution-
graph methods paper (Ameisen et al. 2025) already characterize the canonical
circuits: 2-hop city→state→capital factual recall, entity→sport, simple
two-operand addition, antonym/synonym substitution, acronym expansion,
induction patterns. Avoid variations on these. Generate prompts that go *beyond*. These prompts should be interesting and expansive, not just "swapping in a different idiom or a different long-distance dependency", but should reveal something genuinely worth studying within a model further for a full research paper.

---

# Filter constraints

- 8-30 words in the prompt
- Single-token next word: one English content word, no punctuation
- The expected next word must NOT appear verbatim in the prompt
- Gemma-2-2B must plausibly get the answer right
- top-1 confidence should reasonably be around 0.2 and 0.6

---

# Topic seeds (anchor your prompts in these; cap each at ~1 prompt)

Science & technology, history & civilization, arts & culture, geography &
nature, social sciences, sports & games, music, literature, philosophy.
Phenomena-driven prompts here may benefit from less topical anchoring —
linguistic and pragmatic prompts can be domain-light.

---

# Output format

Produce exactly 10 prompts. Output ONLY the CSV inside one code fence —
explanations go after the fence.

```csv
slug,prompt,intermediate_concept,correct_answer,hop_type,notes
generated-oi-001,Some students passed the exam meaning the rest,scalar implicature,failed,other_interesting_structure,Probes whether "some" triggers a NOT-all supernode separate from positive answer representation
generated-oi-002,...
```

Columns (matching the standard ground_truth CSV schema):
- `slug`: `generated-oi-NNN`
- `prompt`: 8-30 word sentence prefix
- `intermediate_concept`: short tag for the structural phenomenon you
  expect (e.g., "scalar implicature", "self-reference", "type-vs-token",
  "long-distance agreement")
- `correct_answer`: single most likely next content word
- `hop_type`: always `other_interesting_structure`
- `notes`: one sentence on why this structure won't be captured by
  parallel-computation / surprising-supernode / complex-internal-computation
  but is still notable

After the CSV: 1 paragraph on the distribution of structural phenomena and
which ones you think are most likely to produce surprising graph topology.
