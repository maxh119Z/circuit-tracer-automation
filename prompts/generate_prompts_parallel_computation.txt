You are a mechanistic interpretability researcher generating Wikipedia-style
sentence-completion prompts for Gemma-2-2B circuit tracing. Your goal: interesting prompts
whose attribution graphs may show **parallel computation** — the graph contains
distinct supernodes for several different plausible next-token candidates,
not just one.

---

# Target criterion (verbatim from our automated judge)

> **Parallel computation.** The supernodes reflect internal computation toward
> multiple different possible next-token predictions. Trivial variations
> (near-synonyms, different formattings of the same word) do not count. Medium
> scores: supernodes for multiple candidates are present but weakly separated.
> High scores: clearly distinct supernode groups for each candidate.

---

# Design philosophy — avoid retracing the Anthropic biology paper

Anthropic's biology paper (Lindsey et al. 2025) and the original attribution-
graph methods paper (Ameisen et al. 2025) already characterize the canonical
circuits: 2-hop city→state→capital factual recall, entity→sport (Michael
Jordan → basketball), simple two-operand addition, antonym/synonym
substitution, acronym expansion, induction patterns. Avoid variations on
these. Generate prompts that go *beyond*. These prompts should be interesting and expansive, not just "swapping in two near-synonyms" or forcing a token-level competition, but should reveal something genuinely worth studying within a model further for a full research paper.

---

# Filter constraints

- 8-30 words in the prompt
- Single-token next word: one English content word, no punctuation
- The expected next word must NOT appear verbatim in the prompt
- Gemma-2-2B must plausibly get the answer right (so aim for situations
  where multiple plausible continuations are well-known)
- top-1 confidence should reasonably be around 0.2 and 0.6

---

# Topic seeds (anchor your prompts in these; cap each at ~1 prompt)

Science & technology, history & civilization, arts & culture, geography &
nature, social sciences, sports & games, music, literature, philosophy.
Specific anchors: quantum mechanics, neuroscience, Roman history, French

Columns (matching the standard ground_truth CSV schema):

- `prompt`: 8-30 word sentence prefix
- `intermediate_concept`: pipe-separated list of 2+ other plausible next
  tokens you expect the model to internally consider as competing candidates
- `correct_answer`: single most likely next content word
- `hop_type`: always `parallel_computation`
- `notes`: one sentence on why distinct supernodes are likely for each
  competing candidate

After the CSV: 1 paragraph on the distribution of competing-candidate types
